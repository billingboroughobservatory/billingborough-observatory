What does astronomical seeing mean?

Seeing describes the effect of atmospheric turbulence on the apparent sharpness of astronomical objects. Better seeing means steadier air and generally sharper views, particularly when observing the Moon, planets and close double stars.

What to look for

Cloud cover is the first thing to check. Extensive cloud will prevent observations regardless of how good the other conditions are.

Seeing is particularly important for high magnification work. The meteoblue forecast gives predicted seeing in arcseconds; lower values indicate less atmospheric turbulence.

Humidity is also worth watching. High humidity can increase the risk of dew forming on telescope optics and cameras, particularly when temperatures fall towards the dew point.

The forecast is intended as a guide. Conditions can change during the night, and local conditions at the observatory may differ from the forecast.
